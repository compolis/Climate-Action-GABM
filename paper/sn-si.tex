\section{Survey and measurement setup}\label{sec:si-survey}
% Main-text anchors: L180 (Table S1, N=1,483 model-ready), L202/L721 (Table S2).
% All numbers below verified against data/yougov_survey_data/YouGovProcessedData.csv
% via cag.io.survey.load (2026-07-10): N=1,483; age 48.0/17.6 (18-91); male 49.6;
% parent 57.0; White 81.7; GE2019 Con29.0/Lab26.0/LD9.8/Grn3.8/Brx0.9/Oth3.3/DNV27.2;
% EURef Rem43.0/Leave31.7/none25.4; policy baselines as tabulated.

The model is anchored to a YouGov study commissioned by the University of Leeds
and fielded in late April 2024 among UK respondents, with Northern Ireland
excluded from sampling. The source study was an online survey experiment, but the
present model does not use the original treatment assignment as a simulation
lever; instead it uses respondent-level records as anchor data for simulated
citizens. Following the source study, we work with the unweighted
respondent-level data. The source dataset contains $1{,}967$ respondent records.
For agent construction we apply a project-level filter that retains only the rows
with the complete and mappable fields needed for simulation, yielding an analytic
subset of $N=1{,}483$.

\subsection{Analytic subset}\label{subsec:si-analytic-subset}

Each retained respondent row anchors one simulated citizen. The fields used to
build agents include demographic background (age, gender, region, education,
household income, ethnicity, parental status), political history and orientation
(2019 general-election vote, EU referendum vote, left--right self-placement), and
value or ideology measures including self-transcendence, self-enhancement,
openness, conformity--tradition, social dominance orientation, ecological
dominance orientation, and right-wing authoritarianism. These value and political
fields are the same signals that drive the affinity-based audience assignment
described in Section~\ref{subsec:si-structure}.
Table~\ref{tab:si-survey-sample} summarises the filtered subset actually used to
construct agents.

\subsection{Policy measures and scale}\label{subsec:si-policy-measures}

The main outcome variables are six climate-policy items: renewable energy
expansion, a ban on new oil, gas, and coal licences, a 2030 ban on new petrol
cars, stronger standards for new housing, a carbon tax with dividend, and
compensation for people in other countries affected by climate change. The
original survey records each item on a seven-point ordered support--opposition
scale. In the model these seven positions are relabelled A--G, where A means
strongly oppose, B somewhat oppose, C slightly oppose, D neutral, E slightly
support, F somewhat support, and G strongly support, and are then mapped onto a
centered numeric scale from $-3$ to $+3$ (with D~$\to 0$).

Table~\ref{tab:si-policy-baselines} shows the six items in the filtered analytic
subset. All reported runs use the full six-policy package in package mode; the
package index is the arithmetic mean of the six centered policy scores. These
survey responses play two roles in the model: they provide the Day~0
ground-truth anchor for each citizen (Section~\ref{subsec:si-execution-loop}), and
they remain the comparison target in the evaluation analyses
(Section~\ref{sec:si-evaluation}).

\section{Personas and prompts}\label{sec:si-personas}
% Main-text anchors: L188 (Section S2, persona samples), L723-727 (personas +
% message pool + prompt templates incl two-step debiased survey + memory).
% Sources: src/cag/abm/agent.py (get_persona + prompt templates), build_nation

\subsection{Citizen personas}\label{subsec:si-citizen-personas}
% Source: src/cag/abm/agent.py get_persona() (= _build_demographics_text +
% _build_values_text). Examples regenerated via cag.__main__.build_nation on the
% YouGov subset (2026-07-10); Example 1 also appears in Prompts_and_Personas_Guide_v2.md.

Each citizen agent is given a first-person persona built directly from its
anchored YouGov respondent record and placed at the opening of the system prompt.
The persona is the concatenation of two paragraphs: a demographic paragraph (age,
gender, region, ethnicity, education, household income, parental status,
left--right self-placement, 2019 general-election vote, and EU referendum vote)
and a values paragraph rendered from the Schwartz value battery together with the
social-dominance and right-wing-authoritarianism scales. Any attribute recorded as
``don't know'', ``prefer not to say'', or otherwise unmapped is dropped silently
rather than voiced as a confident statement, so persona length varies with how
complete a respondent's record is (typically six to ten sentences). The two
examples below are reproduced verbatim from the persona builder for two
contrasting respondents in the analytic subset.

\paragraph{Example 1 (left-leaning, Remain, Green voter).}
\begin{quote}\small
I am a 32 year old female living in the Wales. My ethnicity is white. I have a
University or CNAA higher degree (e.g.\ M.Sc, Ph.D). My gross household income is
\pounds40,000 -- \pounds44,999 per year. I am not a parent. I position myself
fairly left-wing of the political spectrum. I voted for the Green party candidate
in the 2019 General Election. I voted to remain in the 2016 EU Referendum.

When it comes to my core values and worldview: I care about the people close to me
and have a basic respect for nature, but I do not actively champion global equality
or make environmental protection a primary, driving life focus. I am not strongly
driven by the need to get ahead of others, impress people, or hold leadership
positions. I am highly curious, adventurous, and love trying out new things. I do
not feel strictly bound by traditional values. I strongly believe that all groups
should have an equal chance to succeed. I believe that all lifeforms on Earth
should be treated equally. I am highly skeptical of leaders.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Example 2 (centre, Leave, Brexit-party voter).}
\begin{quote}\small
I am a 58 year old female living in the Wales. My ethnicity is white. I have a GCE
A level or Higher Certificate. My gross household income is under \pounds5,000 per
year. I am a parent. I position myself centre of the political spectrum. I voted
for the Brexit party candidate in the 2019 General Election. I voted to leave in
the 2016 EU Referendum.

When it comes to my core values and worldview: I am deeply committed to caring for
nature, protecting the environment, and responding to the needs of others. I
strongly believe in global harmony, equal opportunities for everyone, and actively
helping those around me. I am not strongly driven by the need to get ahead of
others, impress people, or hold leadership positions where I tell others what to
do. I prefer routine and the familiar, showing little interest in taking risks,
seeking out new adventures, or coming up with highly original ideas. I place a high
value on obedience, showing deep respect for parents and older people. I strongly
believe in maintaining traditional ways of thinking, keeping up customs, and always
behaving properly according to expectations. I strongly believe that all groups
should have an equal chance to succeed and that society should actively work to
equalize conditions. I firmly reject the idea that any group is inferior or should
dominate others. I believe that all lifeforms on Earth should be treated equally
and that no single species, including humans, should dominate the planet. I have a
healthy respect for leaders and traditions but maintain some skepticism; I
generally believe force should be avoided unless dealing with highly specific,
threatening situations.
\end{quote}

The second example illustrates that a persona reflects the respondent's recorded
values, which need not align with their vote: a Leave, Brexit-party voter can still
carry strongly pro-environment and egalitarian Schwartz-value scores.

\subsection{Political-agent message pool}\label{subsec:si-political-briefs}
% Composition verified from data/political_messages/messages_v1.csv +
% sources_v1.csv (2026-07-10): 280 messages (A=140, B=140; policies 1-6 + PACKAGE);
% 228 synthetic (grounded), 52 verbatim/broadcast-ready; 24 real cited source quotes
% (12/side, ~2 per policy per side) from manifestos, Hansard, press, social media.
% NOTE: no numbered table here on purpose -- a captioned table would take the S3
% slot the main text reserves for the affinity-assignment table.

Two committed-minority agents seed climate-policy opinions: a pro-climate side (A)
and a climate-sceptic side (B). Neither updates its position during a run, and
their broadcasts are not generated at run time. Instead each side draws from a
fixed, pre-authored message pool that is held constant across every reported run.

The pool contains 280 messages, split evenly between the two sides (140 each) and
spanning the six individual policies and the six-policy package. The messages are
synthetic --- composed to voice each side's platform in a recognisably partisan
register --- but they are not free invention: each is grounded in real, cited party
communications. The grounding material is a set of 24 real source quotes (twelve
per side, roughly two per policy per side) drawn from party manifestos,
parliamentary speeches recorded in Hansard, and press and social-media statements,
each stored with its speaker, date, venue, and citation. Every synthetic broadcast
records the specific source quotes it is built from, so the pool is auditable back
to its real-world material; a small number of near-verbatim broadcast-ready
messages (one per policy per side) are summarised directly from those sources.

Because message content is held fixed across the reported runs, it is not one of
the resource levers manipulated in the main text; systematically varying it --- its
framing, its emotional intensity, and how much is verbatim-real rather than
synthetic --- is left to future work. The full message pool and its source table
are released as \texttt{messages\_v1.csv} and \texttt{sources\_v1.csv}.

\subsection{Prompt templates used in the reported runs}\label{subsec:si-prompt-stack}
% Verbatim from src/cag/abm/agent.py (2026-07-10), matching
% docs/Prompts_and_Personas_Guide_v2.md. All first-person (v0.5 overhaul); the
% third-person survey variant was tested and dropped.

Citizens always receive a system prompt assembled from persona and memory
(Section~\ref{subsec:si-execution-loop}); the user prompts below are the exact
templates used in every reported run, with placeholders shown in square brackets.
All are first-person. An earlier third-person wording of the survey step was tested
and dropped for worse performance.

\paragraph{Day-0 rationale.}
Under ground-truth-with-rationale anchoring the Day~0 opinion is set directly from
the respondent's survey answer, and the model is asked only to justify that pre-set
position in character:
\begin{quote}\small
Your considered position on the following policy is ``[response label]'':

[policy question]

In 2-3 sentences, explain why, given your background and values, you genuinely hold
this position.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Reflection on a broadcast (package mode).}
Each exposed citizen reads a broadcast and reflects without committing to a
position:
\begin{quote}\small
You just received the following political message about a package of climate
policies:

``[message]''

The package includes:

[bulleted list of all six policy questions]

In a few sentences, reflect on how this affects your thinking about the overall
package. You may mention which parts feel more or less convincing. Do not state a
final position --- just think out loud.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Peer message (package mode).}
Each citizen with at least one network neighbour writes a short conversational
message:
\begin{quote}\small
Express your current thinking about the following climate-policy package in 2-3
sentences. Be genuine and conversational, and feel free to mention if some parts
appeal to you more than others:

[bulleted list of all six policy questions]
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Reflection on received peer messages (package mode).}
\begin{quote}\small
You just received peer messages about a package of climate policies. The package
includes:

[bulleted list of all six policy questions]

Here is what they said:

1. ``[message 1]''
2. ``[message 2]''
\dots

In a few sentences, reflect on how these peer messages affect your thinking about
the overall package. Do not state a final position --- just think out loud.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{End-of-day survey: debiased two-step.}
The reported runs use a two-step debiasing survey. Step~1 elicits reasoning behind
an explicit anti-sycophancy preamble:
\begin{quote}\small
Your task is to faithfully simulate how you would respond as the person described
above, NOT to give the 'correct' or socially desirable answer.

Given your demographic profile, political history, and psychological values, what
factors would shape your view on the following policy?

[policy question]

Consider factors that might lead you to SUPPORT this policy AND factors that might
lead you to OPPOSE it. Think about your voting history, your values, your life
circumstances, and the messages and reflections from today and previous days.

Provide your reasoning in 2-3 sentences.
\end{quote}
Step~2 replays that reasoning into the system prompt and asks for the answer:
\begin{quote}\small
Based on the reasoning above, how would you respond to the following survey
question?

[policy question]

A. Strongly oppose \quad B. Somewhat oppose \quad C. Slightly oppose \quad
D. Neutral \quad E. Slightly support \quad F. Somewhat support \quad
G. Strongly support

Respond with a single letter A-G.
\end{quote}
The response letter maps to the centered $-3$ to $+3$ scale (A~$=-3$, D~$=0$,
G~$=+3$).

\paragraph{Memory compression.}
On longer runs, once a day is older than the previous one, its full reflections and
the agent's own survey reasoning are compressed into a short summary that replaces
the full text in later prompts. The auxiliary call uses the system prompt ``You are
a concise summariser.'' and:
\begin{quote}\small
Concisely summarise the following day in 4-5 first-person sentences. Cover: the
positions you landed on and your key reasoning, which received messages you found
compelling and which you pushed back on, and whether your thinking shifted on any
aspect of the policy.

Day's reflections and your own reasoning:
[memories]
\end{quote}

\section{Model architecture and execution}\label{sec:si-architecture}
% Main-text anchors: L728-730 (Section S3 runtime pipeline; Table S3;

\subsection{Population instantiation}\label{subsec:si-instantiation}
% Source: src/cag/abm/sim.py (data.sample(n=n_citizens, random_state=seed));
% src/cag/presets.py (tier1 preset). N=100, seeds 42/43/44.

Each run instantiates its citizen cohort by drawing a random sample of $N=100$
respondents from the $N=1{,}483$ model-ready subset (Section~\ref{sec:si-survey}),
seeded by the run's random seed so that a given seed always yields the same cohort.
Each sampled record becomes one citizen agent whose attributes (demographics,
political history, and the value and ideology scales) are read directly from the
record and whose persona text is built as in
Section~\ref{subsec:si-citizen-personas}. Because the sample is taken before any
experimental settings are applied, a given seed produces an identical starting
cohort across all four experiments; the reported runs use seeds 42, 43, and 44.

\subsection{Affinity audience assignment and peer network}\label{subsec:si-structure}
% Source: src/cag/abm/config/exposure.py (target presets + affinity-weight tiers),
% environment.py (_assign_affinity_rank, _green/_reform_affinity_score),
% networks.py (_build_stochastic_block + Barabasi-Albert builder).

Two structures connect the agents, and they are assigned separately: which
committed minority each citizen hears (the broadcast audience), and which peers each
citizen exchanges reflections with (the social network).

\paragraph{Broadcast audience.}
Each citizen receives an affinity score for each side, computed from its survey
record as a weighted sum of three tiers of signals: political self-report
(left--right placement, EU referendum vote, and 2019 general-election vote),
psychological values (the Schwartz value battery together with right-wing
authoritarianism and social dominance orientation), and demographics (age,
education, region). The political tier carries the largest weight and demographics
the smallest (Table~\ref{tab:si-exposure-rules}). Rather than let audience sizes
fall out of the politically-engaged YouGov sample, we set them to calibrated target
shares drawn from UK population-level evidence and fill each cell by ranking
citizens on their affinity scores: the sceptic-only cell is locked first (its
demographic signature is sharpest), then the pro-climate-only cell, then the
cross-cutting ``both'' cell, with the remainder assigned to ``neither''. Under the
symmetric default used in the reported runs the realised split is 5\%
pro-climate-only, 5\% sceptic-only, 60\% both, and 30\% neither
(Table~\ref{tab:si-exposure-rules}). The legacy voting-history assignment rule of
earlier versions is retained in the code for reproducibility but is not used in the
reported runs.

\paragraph{Peer network.}
Citizens are additionally linked in a social network, by default a two-block
stochastic block model with denser within-block than between-block ties
($p_{\mathrm{intra}}=0.15$, $p_{\mathrm{inter}}=0.05$). The two blocks are keyed on
the broadcast-exposure structure, but because the cross-cutting ``both'' and
disengaged ``neither'' citizens (together about 90\% of the population) are split
across both blocks, the graph is only weakly sorted by exposure rather than a
strict echo chamber. On each simulated day every citizen relays its reflection to a
fixed number of randomly chosen neighbours ($k=2$), regardless of how well
connected it is. The targeting experiment (Section~\ref{subsec:si-reported-runs})
replaces the stochastic block model with a scale-free Barab\'asi--Albert graph
($m=5$) so that degree-based targeting has hubs to aim at.

\subsection{Day~0 anchoring and the daily execution loop}\label{subsec:si-execution-loop}
% Source: src/cag/abm/sim.py (day loop), config/memory.py (day0_anchor.ttl_days=1),
% agent.py (seed_opinion_with_rationale, two-step administer_survey).

At Day~0 each citizen's opinion on every policy is pinned directly to its
respondent's observed survey answer (ground-truth-with-rationale anchoring), and the
agent generates a short first-person rationale for each seeded answer
(Section~\ref{subsec:si-prompt-stack}); no end-of-day survey is run on Day~0. This
makes the Day~0 cohort mean equal to the YouGov mean by construction, so any later
movement is attributable to the simulated interactions rather than to the model's
own prior.

Each subsequent simulated day runs a fixed sequence of phases: the exposed citizens
receive one or more broadcasts from the committed minorities and write a private
reflection after each, then exchange peer messages with their $k$ neighbours and
reflect again, and finally answer the six-policy end-of-day survey through the
two-step debiased prompt (Section~\ref{subsec:si-prompt-stack}). Citizens who hear
both sides receive the two broadcasts in a randomised order that varies day to day.
The Day~0 rationale is carried in the prompt only briefly: under the memory
configuration used in the reported runs its time-to-live is one day
($\texttt{day0\_anchor.ttl\_days}=1$), so from roughly the second simulated day
onward the anchor drops out of context and an agent's answers are driven by its
recent reflections and daily summaries rather than by a standing reminder of its
starting position.

\subsection{Outputs, persistence, reproducibility, and decoding settings}\label{subsec:si-outputs}
% Source: src/cag/abm/sim.py SIM_CONFIG (llm_temperature=0.5, thinking=False),
% scripts/aire/run.sh (vLLM serve Qwen/Qwen3-14B), io/results.py + io/checkpoint.py.

Every generative step is produced by a single local open-weight language model,
Qwen3-14B, served locally (through vLLM on the HPC backend) and shared by all
citizen roles --- reflections, peer messages, Day-0 rationales, and end-of-day
survey answers. Decoding uses a temperature of $0.5$ with the model's optional
extended-thinking mode disabled; committed-minority broadcasts are not generated at
run time but drawn from the fixed pool of
Section~\ref{subsec:si-political-briefs}. Because model outputs are stochastic,
every condition is run under three random seeds (42, 43, 44), which also fix the
cohort sample and all network and exposure draws, so a run is reproducible from its
seed and configuration.

Each run writes a set of machine-readable outputs: per-agent Day-0 and end-of-day
opinions, the full reflection and message logs, the assigned exposure cell and
affinity scores per agent, and the fully resolved run configuration. Long runs
support checkpoint-and-resume. The provenance notebooks that recompute every number
in the main text and in Tables~\ref{tab:si-outcomes-reach-freq}
and~\ref{tab:si-outcomes-target-iso} read directly from these per-run outputs.

\subsection{Experiment configurations and outcomes}\label{subsec:si-reported-runs}

The four experiments share the base configuration above --- an $N=100$ cohort
anchored to the YouGov subset, package mode, ground-truth-with-rationale Day~0
anchoring with a one-day anchor time-to-live, active peer exchange ($k=2$), the
committed-minority exposure structure, Qwen3-14B at temperature $0.5$, and three
seeds --- and differ only in the lever each isolates.
Table~\ref{tab:si-run-config} gives the per-experiment settings,
Table~\ref{tab:si-run-baselines} the anchored Day~0 baselines, and
Tables~\ref{tab:si-outcomes-reach-freq} and~\ref{tab:si-outcomes-target-iso} the
per-condition outcomes. Every outcome is a within-person difference in the final
package index (a positive value favours the pro-climate side), pooled over the

\subsection{Estimand, effect sizes, and ceiling-robust rulers}\label{subsec:si-estimand}

Because every condition pins each agent's Day~0 opinion to the same observed survey
value, the quantity we report for a manipulation is a within-person paired
difference: the change in a citizen's final package index under one condition minus
its change under the comparison condition. With a common anchored starting point the
difference-in-differences reduces to this paired endpoint difference, so the paired
statistic is the natural estimand and its common-mode component (including the
model's level bias, Section~\ref{sec:grounding}) cancels. We summarise it by its
mean, a 95\% confidence interval, and a paired/permutation test; standardised effect
sizes (Cohen's $d_z$) accompany each contrast in the provenance notebooks. The
five-day reach validation contrast, for example, is $+0.42$ package-index points (95\%
CI $[+0.34, +0.51]$, $p\approx1\times10^{-19}$, $n=300$ over three seeds), and is
positive in all three seed cohorts ($+0.53$, $+0.42$, $+0.31$); the seven-day reach
experiment (Table~\ref{tab:si-outcomes-reach-freq}) gives a consistent $+0.35$.

A bounded $-3$ to $+3$ scale could in principle manufacture an apparent asymmetry:
agents already near the ceiling cannot move further up. To rule this out we re-express
the same final opinions on rulers that do not share the raw scale's fixed ceiling and
re-measure the reach effect (Table~\ref{tab:si-rulers}). Every ruler returns a
positive effect, so the result is not an artefact of the bounded scale. The
transformed magnitudes are not comparable across rulers --- each transform carries
its own units --- so only their common sign is interpretable; the pure-ordinal rank
ruler is the most conservative and remains positive.

\subsection{Persona signal and residual bias}\label{sec:grounding}

\paragraph{Does the model read the persona?}
We test this with a permutation design at Day~0, before any messages, across three
persona arms (three seeds each). With the real persona the model orders citizens
close to their actual survey answers (Spearman $\rho\approx0.62$ on the package
index, $\approx0.42$ averaged over the six policies). When each agent is instead
given another respondent's persona (a derangement), the correlation with its own
respondent's answers collapses to near zero ($\rho\approx-0.10$): the agent adopts
the stranger's leanings rather than its own. With an information-free neutral
persona the ordering collapses entirely ($\rho\approx0.02$), leaving Day~0 opinion a
near-flat function of the policy question alone. The model is therefore genuinely
reading the persona it is handed.

\paragraph{Residual level bias.}
Reading the persona is not the same as reading it without bias. Without anchoring,
the model sits about $+0.64$ package-index points above the true survey mean, and
the inflation is uneven across policies (Table~\ref{tab:si-bias}): largest on the
cost-framed and behaviour-change items (Ban petrol cars $+1.64$, Carbon tax $+0.96$,
Climate compensation $+0.68$) and small on Renewable energy ($+0.33$), Ban fossil
fuels ($+0.36$), and Green housing (slightly negative). This is exactly why the
reported runs anchor each agent's Day~0 answer to its observed survey value: the
anchor removes this level bias by construction, and the within-person paired
differences of Section~\ref{subsec:si-estimand} cancel whatever common-mode bias
remains, so no result rests on an absolute level.

\subsection{Reliability across graph and model}\label{subsec:si-reliability}

Finally, we check that the reach effect is not specific to arbitrary modelling
choices. Across three social-network topologies the effect is same-signed and
strongly significant, replicated over three seeds (pooled $n=300$; all
$p<10^{-10}$): $+0.42$ on the stochastic block model, $+0.34$ on a scale-free
Barab\'asi--Albert graph, and $+0.36$ on a small-world Watts--Strogatz graph; the
confidence intervals overlap, so the effect is topology-robust. It also survives a
change of language model: re-running the contrast on Llama-3.1-8B reproduces a
same-signed, strongly significant difference ($+1.48$, single seed). That second
model is a demonstrably poorer simulator --- it holds the persona ordering only
weakly ($\rho\approx0.46$) and reads systematically low rather than high --- yet the
contrast persists, because the within-person difference cancels each model's
common-mode error. The difference-engine design is credible precisely because the
effect survives on a model that gets absolute opinions wrong; the single-seed
cross-model run supports a directional claim only, not a cross-draw magnitude.
