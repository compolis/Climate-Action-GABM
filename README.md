# Climate-Action-GABM
A generative agent-based modelling framework (LLM+ABM) for social simulation. The framework is designed for the following goal: The project seeks to understand the dynamics of social tipping or polarization under the influence of competing persuasion from two competing committed minorities with opposing goals.

### Project abstract

#### Generative Agent-based model (GABM) on simulating the effects of two competing committed minorities on the majority’s tipping

The project seeks to understand the dynamics of social tipping or polarization under the influence of competing persuasion from two competing committed minorities with opposing goals. Traditionally, agent-based modelling (ABM) relied on simplified interaction rules. Recent advancements in Generative Artificial Intelligence (genAI), however, enable a new class of generative agent-based models in which agents are endowed with human-like personas and powered by Large Language Models (LLMs). These “talking agents” can engage in natural-language conversations—debating, persuading, or disagreeing—on issues of interest. Through such exchanges, agents may revise their individual stances, and these local shifts can cascade through the social network to shape collective attitudes. We envision the agents playing a coordination (social-tipping) game with persuasive communication, where they need to choose one of two options (climate action, no action). One committed minority group (representing climate action advocates such as green political parties, campaign groups, climate movements, etc.) will always choose climate action (unless the majority, excluding them, has chosen no action) and will try to persuade the majority through communication to choose climate action. The other committed minority group (representing climate-denial/delay political parties and grassroots groups) will always choose no action (unless the majority, excluding them, has chosen climate action) and will try to persuade the majority through communication to choose no action. We aim to use our own representative survey data to construct the majority agents, so the agents are heterogeneous with respect to socio-demographics, values, political affiliation, climate change, and climate policies attitudes, etc.
